## Flagged decisions

**F-1. Procedure practice is "put the steps in order".** *Why:* the research gave every procedure an ordered list of steps, so this needs no new content and works with a stylus tap. *Alternatives:* multiple-choice "what comes next", or scenes with a virtual meter (the real trainer, FR-6, still needs scenes). *Reverse if:* you want tests of reasoning, not recall of sequence.

**F-2. Difficulty sets how many steps are dealt:** Apprentice 4, Journeyman 6, Master all, as a run of consecutive steps from a seeded start. Score is the share of neighbouring pairs in the right order, so one swap keeps most credit. Evidence goes to every skill the procedure uses, at the tier of the difficulty. *Alternative:* all-or-nothing scoring.

**F-3. Practice is offered for 28 of 44 procedures,** those whose every skill is built. The other 16 are listed (with steps, hazards and controls) but cannot be practised because each needs a phase 3 or 3 skill that does not exist yet (11 of them have no coverage at all). *Alternative:* offer practice for all and let the phase-3 skills be missing.

**F-4. Only phase 1 occupations are "available"** (superseded 2026-10-13: the MVP covers all six, so all six are available, D-19) (electricians, helpers, inspectors); solar, alarm and power-line show as coming soon and cannot be chosen. *Why:* you accepted the three phases; marketing must not promise the rest. *Alternative:* open all six now (the content exists, the skills do not).

**F-5. Utility interface is a phase-1 skill.** Your list of phase 1 had seven skills; I built all seven, including utility interface, though it is the least used. Lesson only.

**F-6. Student text is cleaned of code and agency names** (NEC, OSHA, UL, IEEE, ICC and others) by replacement rules in `build_app_data.py`, and the build fails if any remain. The validator and a test also reject them. I rewrote 9 passages (for example, "the NEC forbids it" became "the code forbids it"). The research documents keep the names; students never see them. *Check:* read the 9 replacements in `REPLACE` for meaning.

**F-7. Occupation summaries are written for students in our own words** (the research summaries name agencies). They are in `SUMMARY` in the build script.

**F-8. Occupation is a pathway, not a credential.** The choice is saved, and it changes what the app suggests; skill progress is never tied to it. *Alternative:* filter progress by occupation.

**F-9. 13 of the 24 "none" procedures, those that needed only phase-1 skills, were upgraded to "partial".** The wording says the app teaches the sequence and the safety reasoning, not that it trains the work hands-on. *Alternative:* keep them "none" until a scene exists. This is the most generous reading; the "partial" count rises from 18 to 31 because of it. The old rating is kept in `appCoverageBefore` in the data.

**F-10. All new lessons and occupation data are `draft`.** Nothing is marked reviewed. They need an occupational expert (your standing item).

**F-11. Compile-check stub:** I added `interactable` to the `Selectable` stub in `tools/unity-compile-check/Stubs.cs`; the real Unity class has it.

**F-12. Companion standards (2026-10-13).** The fire alarm standard and the line work rules are profiles that supplement the NEC (`supplements` in `profile.json`), not codes a student picks. A student whose region uses the NEC also gets their entries, searchable and citable, and violations that cite them. This needed a small C# change (`ProfileSupplements`, `CompositeCodeProfile`, `CodeProfiles.CitationIdsFor`) because the active profile was a single code. The alternative was copying those entries into each NEC profile. Say if you would rather have that.

**F-13. The utility profile follows the US federal line-work rules, not the NESC.** The federal text is public and could be checked paragraph by paragraph; the NESC is paywalled. A NESC profile can be added later as another companion.
